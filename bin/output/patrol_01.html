Subject: U.S.S. PAMPANITO - Report of First War Patrol.

(A) PROLOGUE

This ship was placed in commission at the U.S. Navy Yard, Portsmouth, New Hampshire, on 6 November 1943. Training was commenced 29 November 1943 in the Portsmouth, Newport, New London area. During the period 29 November 1943 to 15 January 1944 conducted the following: boat trials, practice approaches, 6-day practice patrol, fired contract torpedo trials, dock and sound tested, fired 3 exercise torpedoes, test fired all guns, calibrated pitometer log, in drydock a total of 11 days undergoing repairs to propeller shaft bearings.

Departed New London, Connecticut on 15 January 1944. Made transit of Panama Canal on 24 January 1944. In Balboa, Canal Zone area from 26 January to 29 January 1944 for minor repairs and sound tests. Arrived Pearl Harbor, T.H. on 14 February 1944. Base repairs from 14 February 1944 to 22 February 1944 including a 5-day period in ARD #1 drydock for repairs to propeller shaft bearings. Commenced final training 23 February 1944. Conducted torpedo approaches, night dummy motor runs, fired 4 exercise torpedoes, was sound tested and received indoctrination depth charging.

On 6 March 1944 in accordance with orders of ComSubPac, Lieutenant Commander Paul E. Summers, U. S. Navy, relieved Lieutenant Commander Charles B. Jackson, Jr., U.S. Navy, as commanding officer. Commenced additional training period of 6 days for new commanding officer. Made practice approaches, night and day radar rehearsal runs, and fired 6 exercise torpedoes.

Major work accomplished in Pearl Harbor Area was as follows:

(1) Renewed woods and rebored inboard bearings on both propeller shafts.

(2) Completed installations for charging and ventilating Mk. 18 torpedoes in both torpedo rooms.

(3) Installed the following: Mark Island type nickel eliminator for battery water; baffle plate in #1 M.B.T. to decrease flooding time; RSN-3 Radio receiver; voice modulation equipment for TBL-7 radio transmitter; C10 radar detector; IFF radio equipment; variable speed training power for SJ radar mast.

(4) The ship was painted with dark gray camouflage color on vertical surfaces.

Training was completed on 12 March 1944 and loading completed on 14 March 1944, with 16 MK. 14, MOD. 3A torpedoes in forward torpedo room and 8 MK. 18 torpedoes in after torpedo room.

1930 (L)Stopped. Put diver over the side forward to inspect outer door of #6 torpedo tube. Diver reported both gasket and seat in satisfactory condition. Lock to #6 tube must be inside the tube.

Subject: U.S.S. ATANITO - Report of First War Patrol.

1356 (K)Set up looks fairly good, came left to 110° for about 75° starboard track on leading target. The escort is crossing over toward us from port to starboard bow of target. A second look disclosed a small mast well astern and to the starboard of second ship in column. Probably another escort.
1330 (K)With track of about 75° starboard, zero gyro angle and torpedo run of about 1400 yards, up periscope for final check on set-up, disclosing the following: leading escort was heading almost directly for us at range of less than 500 yards, leading target was zigging away to the left, with smoke billowing from his stack. And at
1331 (K)Received one depth charge; not close. Decided to go deep and try to run under target and get on other side of formation for a possible attack on second target, as it would have been wasting torpedoes, had I let go at the leading target with the set-up changing so rapidly and with target heading away. Very puzzled over the fact that the escort was able to pick me up, in that I was very cautious in the amount of periscope exposed and the duration of my exposure was held to a minimum. Think perhaps he may have picked up air noise while we were making the tubes ready, else he is very good at echo ranging.
1345 (K)Started up to periscope depth. Sound reported escort astern, with second target on our port quarter.
1348 (K)Received 2 depth charges fairly close to starboard and above. Went deep.
1403 - 1405 (K)Received a string of 5 depth charges varying in depth from about 150 to 600 feet, all very close with two straddles. During this interval we heard the screws of one escort through our hull, and the sound man distinctly made out a definite ping similar to a fathometer as the escort passed over us and let go his charges. This string of 5 depth charges caused considerable damage and shaking up of the boat as evidenced by inspections later. Damages are listed under Major Defects and Damages.
1406 (K)We can definitely hear two escorts now pinging on us.
1407 - 1600 (K)Continued evasive tactics at deep submergence with both escorts coming in and going out intermittently. We have been unable to locate a definite gradient at any depth thus far.
1600 (K)Sound reported high speed screws moving out, so started up slowly to periscope depth. Trim very heavy at present, but think it unwise to use trim pump until I take a look.

8 April 1944

0206 (K)Still have not picked up convoy after having advanced to his possible position on the 230° - 240° course lines. Possibly the convoy changed base course after dark and headed for Woleal.

Decided to head north and patrol Saipan-Palau route.
0304 (K)Sent contact report to Comsubpac.
0600 (K)Dived to routine torpedoes forward and aft.

Upon pulling torpedoes forward, we found the following: torpedo in #2 tube had flooded exploder; torpedoes in tubes #5 and #6 had flooded after bodies, other torpedoes were dry.

All torpedoes forward were made ready for firing yesterday and the pressure in the tubes of those torpedoes which were flooded was never greater than 20 to 25 lbs. Cannot explain the fact of their flooding except that the shock from the depth charging may have been enough to break the exhaust valve seals. In order to properly routine the flooded after bodies on torpedoes in #5 and #6 tubes, will have to submerge again tomorrow to complete the job.

Inspection of the torpedo s in the after tubes disclosed that the shock from yesterday's depth charging had put two cracks in a section of the battery cells of one Mk. 18 torpedo.

Subject: U.S.S. TARANTO - Report of First War Patrol.

Stationed the radar tracking party and commenced tracking. Found convoy to consist of two large ships with 4 possible escorts: one escort ahead, one on either flank and one astern. The two large ships seem to be in column, zig-zagging on base course about 195° T., with a speed of 9 knots.

0400 (K)Commenced end around.
0407 (K)Observed both targets to be smoking at frequent intervals.
0503 (K)First track of dawn. True bearing of convoy 275° T., range 10,500 yards. Will have to open out and try to get ahead for a daylight attack.

Tracked the two targets out to 34,000 yards with radar, so they are evidently very large AK's.

0714 (K)In position ahead of convoy, range about 35,000 yards. Dived. Went deep to find an injection temperature gradient but could find none down to 400 feet.
0925 (K)At depth of 50 feet, maintaining periscope and sound watch.
1020 (K)Sound picked up echo-ranging bearing about 340° T.
1030 (K)Periscope sighted smoke bearing 342° T. Commenced approach. As convoy came in sight, made out the following: two large 7000 ton freighters (similar to TUYAMA MARU and TOKAI MARU), Force 208 and 226 respectively of ONI 208-J) in column, zig-zagging similarly, closely using short zigs, with a roving destroyer screen on either flank of target group, and a similar destroyer using constant helm about 1500-3000 yards ahead of target group, plus a subcheaser astern of targets. All 3 destroyers were similar to FUBUKI type.
1128 (K)With target group dead ahead, angle on bow zero and with leading destroyer at range of about 5,000 yards, went to 100 feet to pass underneath this screen, and knock clear, to come right for a 90 track, stern shot at leading target. However once at this depth and after coming right for a stern shot, the leading destroyer dropped back on the port bow of leading target, making two screens on this side. According to sound, both of these DD's were moving back and forth ahead and astern of us, and I was gradually opening out from the track. So at --
1144 (K)With one DD still between us and the targets, decided to come left for a bow shot on second freighter in column.
1150 (K)At periscope depth, convoy had zigged away presenting about 150° angle on the bow with a range greater than 6,000 yards.

Decided to wait until range opened out sufficiently to surface and then make an end around and get in an attack after dark.

Excessive noise being caused by the pressure forcing water into main induction piping around the leaky flange rubber gaskets.

0603 (K)Dived. Patrolling north covering Guam-Palau route.
Located several leaks today in flange fittings of outboard air induction piping. Will try to repair these tonight.
Unable to pull MK. 18 torpedo from #9 tube. Will investigate that further.
1845 (K)Surfaced. Commenced work on leaky flanges to outboard air induction piping. Found loose bolts on several flanges. Tightened these. Will test this again tomorrow to see if we did any good in stopping leaks.
By use of a "Jary Rig" we finally pulled the MK. 18 torpedo from #9 tube. The entire torpedo was completely flooded, with warhead casing "Dished in" in several places; battery cells cracked, deformed and completely flooded.
Battery cells are beyond salvaging, so threw them overboard. Other parts of torpedo will be properly cleaned up and preserved as best possible and retained on board.
The armatures and field coils of both sound head training motors have zero grounds and open circuits. So will be unable to use them the remainder of patrol. However, have hopes of trying our lathe motor as a sound head training motor. It may work.

Subject: U.S.S. PAMPANITO - Report of First War Patrol.

(C) WEATHER

In general, the weather in area was very good. Frequent light rain squalls were experienced. The wind was consistently from ENE, about force 2. During morning and evening twilight, the sky was from 50% to 60% overcast, clearing up during remainder of day and night, with visibility usually very good.

(D) TIDAL INFORMATION

A westerly to northwesterly current of from 0.4 to 0.8 knots was prevalent in area.

A southwesterly current of about 0.6 was experienced along eastern side of Yap and as it rounded the southern tip of the island changed its direction to west northwest.

Sea conditions were light to moderate.

(E) NAVIGATIONAL AIDS

Yap Island

No navigational lights were observed. Satisfactory fixes were obtained using tangents and peaks. A very prominent land mark on the eastern side of the island on the hill above Pelck entrance is a large white tower. The approximate position of this tower is Lat. 9° - 31' - .6 N., Long. 138° - 11' - .4 E.

Subject: U.S.S. PAMPANITO (SS383) - Report of first
war patrol - Comments on.

1. The first war patrol of the PAMPANITO covered a
period of fifty-five days, of which thirty were spent in the
assigned area and on Special Lifeguard Mission. The patrol
was terminated because of fuel limitations.

2. Only two contacts worthy of torpedo fire were
made.

Contact 1 - Consisted of two small AKs of about 4,000 tons
zigging radically with one escort, Chidori type, ranging ahead
and one escort ranging astern. A submerged day periscope ap-
proach was made and a good firing position obtained. On rais-
ing periscope for final check the Chidori was observed less
than 500 yards range headed directly for submarine. Depth
charging forced the submarine to evade at deep submergence.
Subsequent pursuit was made but contact was not regained.

Attack 1 - Consisted of two 7,000 ton AKs with three Fubuki
type destroyer escorts, one constant-helming ahead of the
convoy 1,500 to 3,000 yards and one on either flank. A submerged
day periscope approach was made but good firing position was not
attained because of alert escorts and evasive measures taken by
convoy. An end-around was made during the night and a submerged
night radar approach begun from ahead. Four bow torpedoes fired,
range 2,400 yards, on leading target, 200 percent target cover-
age. Two timed hits, one of which was a very violent explosion.
The target's screws were reported stopped by sound. The remain-
ing two bow torpedoes were fired at one of the escorts, range
2,400 yards. No hits were heard or observed. Depth charging
forced the submarine to deep submergence.
Damage assessment - One AK, 7,000 tons, damage.

3. Thirty four air contacts were made which gave the
PAMPANITO considerable training in diving the boat. The skill
manifested by the crew in the repair of casualties sustained as
