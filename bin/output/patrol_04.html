(A) PROLOGUE

Arrived Submarine Base, Pearl Harbor, T.H. from Third War Patrol on 28 September 1944 for normal refit by Submarine Base, Pearl Harbor, T.H. and Submarine Division 281 Relief Crew. Major jobs accomplished were: Overhaul of three main and the auxiliary engines; installation of Mark 8 TBT's; replacement of all type SAE 1045 USN motor brushes with old type SAE 1045; installation of Navy type VHF; new slotted SJ reflector; APR with oscilloscope; Gould trim pump; new all steel rectangle gasket hatches in all positions except forward torpedo room, gun trunk and conning tower (hatches not available for these three openings).

On 7 October 1944, Captain FRANK W. FENNO, JR., USN. relieved Commander PAUL E. SUMMERS, USN, as Commanding Officer. Training period 17 to 25 October 1944. Loading 26-27 October 1944. Readiness for sea on 28 October 1944.

Subject: U.S.S. PAMPANITO (SS383) - Report of War Patrol No. Four.

1. During the fourth war patrol of the PAMPANITO the Commanding Officer, Captain F. W. FENNO, U.S. Navy acted as commander of a coordinated search and attack group operating off Hainan. During the patrol three torpedo attacks were made, all with Mark 18 torpedoes.

2. Attack No. 1 was made on 1 November 1944 on a convoy consisting of two large freighters and an escort. Six bow tubes were fired at the leading freighter in a night surface radar attack, from good position. Two hits were observed which finished off this ship. The PAMPANITO swung and fired four stern shots at the second freighter. One hit was heard which caused a small fire. Torpedoes used incomplete coverage of 600 feet and 425 feet respectively. The second freighter was subsequently attacked by another submarine of the pack but the attack was unsuccessful and the freighter and escort made for the coast.

3. Attack No. 2 on 30 November was a periscope attack at dawn after a five hour end around in bright moonlight against a high speed convoy of four ships and three escorts. A chance in base course put the PAMPANITO well off the track and an estimated 4000 yard range had to be accepted. Three torpedoes were fired at the leading ship, and three at the second ship in column, both medium tankers. Spread used covered 900 feet in both cases. There were no visible results of the attack, but five explosions were heard which could have been hits at extreme range or end of run explosions.

4. Attack No. 3 on 3 December was a coordinated attack on a convoy of 1 merchantman, one convoy was successfully attacked by other submarines of the pack during the night. PAMPANITO tracked for one and one half hours and made a periscope attack at dawn. The four after tubes were fired at a large tanker. The PAMPANITO forced deep by escorts before the bow tubes could be brought to bear and before results of the attack could be observed. Again explosions were heard which could have been hits at extreme range or end of run explosions. This attack was made in very heavy seas, but attack No. 2 was made in a dead calm sea. In attack No. 3 the range was estimated as 3960 yards, 45° track, spread covered 900 feet.

1. The Fourth War Patrol of the PAMPANITO was conducted
in Commander Submarines Pacific Areas of interest, as a member
of a coordinated search and attack group consisting of PAMPANITO
(CTC), SEALION and PIPEFISH.

2. PAMPANITO is the fourth submarine to be commanded
in this war by Captain P. E. SUMMERS and this patrol was conducted
in the well planned and effective manner which has characterized
all of this veteran submarine commander's previous patrols. After
observing the approach tactics throughout three attacks on this
Area, the Commanding Officer decided to fully exploit the advantages
of surface patrol during daylight. Although fifty aircraft contacts
were made and PAMPANITO was forced down forty two times, the
prevailing high state of training and its resulting alertness
prevented her from being bombed a single time.

3. The first attack on the eighteenth of November, was
a night radar attack on a three ship convoy which was not zig-
zagging. Six bow and four stern tubes were fired and two hits were
observed to sink one ship and one hit was observed to damage
another. The second attack was made on a freighter during bright
moonlight which necessitated a submerged periscope approach. Six
torpedoes were fired at two targets, but no hits were observed.
The third attack, on 9 December was conducted submerged in very
rough seas upon a convoy which consisted of four ships and several
escorts. The four stern tubes were fired at a range 20 and one hit
damaged the target.

4. All of the assessed damage took place in Areas under
the Operational Control of Commander Submarines Pacific Fleet.
