Subject: U. S. S. PAMPANITO - Report of Third War Patrol

A) PROLOGUE

Arrived Submarine Base, Midway Island, T.H. from Second War Patrol on 23 July 1944 for normal refit by U.S.S. PROTEUS and Submarine Division 262 Relief Crew. Stood in ARD-8 from 3 to 5 August 1944. Major work items accomplished during refit were:

1. Removed gaskets in conning tower hatch, main induction hull valve and No. 1 flood valve in No. 4A Fuel Ballast Tank.

(2) Renewed all main motor brushes with new "USN" type.

(3) Installed radio key in SJ radar circuit for communications.

(4) Completed installing MK. 18 charging equipment in forward torpedo room.

Underway sound tests conducted 8 August 1944. Underway training exercises conducted 9 to 14 August 1944. Loading accomplished 14-15 August 1944. On 12 August 1944, Lieutenant (jg) William L. Bruckart, D-V(RS), USNR was detached and Lieutenant(jg) Richard J. Sherlock, D-V(RS), USNR reported on board for duty. On 15 August 1944, Ensign Percy E. Pike, USN, was detached because of illness and Ensign Charles K. Bartholomew, D-V(S), USNR reported on board for duty on 16 August 1944. Readiness for sea on 15 August 1944. Two day delay due to a casualty to #4 main motor. A section of the compensating winding shorted across the series field winding resulting in burned insulation, burned brushes, and much loose carbon scattered throughout the motor; cause not definitely determined. The damage was repaired by the tender on 15-16 August 1944. Underway for 3rd War Patrol on 17 August 1944.

B) NARRATIVE

17 August 1944

0750 (Y)Underway from Midway Island, T.H. with air escort enroute to area in company with U.S.S. GROWLER (SS215) and U.S.S. SEALION (SS315) as a coordinated attack group, in accordance with ComTaskFor Seventeen Operation Order 272-44 of 10 August 1944, and Commander Task Group 17.17 supplementary patrol instructions. OTC Lieutenant Commander T.B. OAKLEY in U.S.S. GROWLER.
0905 (Y)Made deep dive; everything satisfactory.
0930 (Y)Surfaced and set course for area at two-engine speed. Enroute to area held; daily training dives for new lookouts and officers, problems for fire control party, emergency drills and instruction periods for all unqualified men.
1100 (Y)Participated in communication exercises for wolf pack and found VHF not very satisfactory. Will work on it.

18 August 1944

Crossed International Date Line and dropped this day from calendar.

2243 (I)Fired four stern tubes; two at each of the two AK's in farthest column. A short interval after the seven hits, the escorts started dropping depth charges at random, but for once we didn't mind.

13 September 1944

0010 (I)Radar contact 053° T., range 14,600 yards. Two possible small pips. Looked as though they might be escort vessels. Tracking showed one to be stopped (evidently picking up survivors), while the other one was maneuvering around at slow speed. Commenced closing to attack the one that was stopped. 0030 (I)Heard much chatter on 500 kc. and noted that our target was now making about 5 or 6 knots. Decided to attack as soon as possible and got clear.
0050 (I)Fired three torpedoes at near target. No hits. At time of firing, the other target was at a range of about 10,000 yards, still showing a fairly small pip. Thus I assumed him to be another escort, since the night was very dark and I could not make out any outline of his silhouette at that range. Later developments however, lead me to believe that this second pip was that of one of the two damaged AK's and must have been very low in the water - because a day later a blazing ship was sighted here by the SEALION and the day following by us. Anyway, thinking they were both small escorts, and since there were no other pips on the radar screen, decided not to waste any more torpedoes on them, so pulled clear to the southeast to join up with the "pack" by the following night.

Subject:

U.S.S. PAMPANITO (SS323) - Report of THIRD War Patrol.

1. The third war patrol of PAMPANITO, as a member of a coordinated attack group which also included U.S.S. GROWLER (Lieutenant Commander T.B. OAKLEY J.T.O.) and U.S.S. SEA LION, was brief, spectacular and effective. Of forty-two (42) days on patrol seventeen (17) were spent in the assigned area, LUZON STRAITS and SOUTH CHINA SEAS.

2. The only contact worthy of torpedo fire was encountered on 12 September. The group made radar contact with a large convoy at 0130. While maneuvering for position PAMPANITO observed attacks by GROWLER at 0155 and SEA LION at 0527. Further action on the part of the enemy made a long day light chase necessary. Air cover forced PAMPANITO to submerge at 1359 and again at 1516. As a consequence contact was lost at a very critical moment in view of the enemy's established habit of making a radical alteration of base course at dusk. However, thoughtful estimate of enemy's probably movement resulted in contact being established at 2106. At this time the remnant of the convoy consisted of one large transport, two large and one medium cargo ships and an undetermined number of escorts.

3. PAMPANITO maneuvered to obtain a position which afforded favorable track angles with moderate torpedo run and small gyro angles and attacked all principal ships with nine torpedoes. Hits were observed in three of the targets and a timed hit was heard but not seen in the fourth. The large transport and one large cargo ship were seen to sink. The second large cargo ship caught fire and was observed, abandoned and still burning on 14 September.

4. After reloading PAMPANITO closed the scene of action and made radar contact with two widely separated targets. One of these, identified as an escort, was unsuccessfully attacked with three torpedoes from a firing position that cannot be called the best. Torpedo run was nearly extreme, and the spread used was excessive. It is probable that these factors rather than faulty depth performance caused the miss.
