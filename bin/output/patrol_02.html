Subject: U.S.S. PAMPANITO - Report of Second War Patrol.

(A) PROLOGUE

Arrived Submarine Base, Pearl Harbor, T.H. from First War Patrol on 8 May 1944 for normal refit by Submarine Base and Submarine Division 42 Relief Crew. Drydocked in ARD #1 from 9 to 12 May 1944. Major work items accomplished during refit were:

(1) Conversion of #4 MBT to FBT.
(2) Rearrangement of officer' country with installation of two additional bunks.
(3) Replacement of rubber flange gaskets in main induction piping in superstructure with Consolco gaskets.
(4) Renewed gaskets and realigned all outer torpedo tube doors while in drydock.
(5) Installation of DRT in conning tower.
(6) Installation of circulating water pump for cooling engines while submerged.
(7) Installation of two (2) high pressure globe stop valves in engine room circulating salt water system.
(8) Removal of CUD and installation of APR.
(9) Installation of VHF receiver and transmitter.

Underway sound tests and training exercises were conducted 27 - 31 May 1944. Loading accomplished 1 - 2 June. One day extension granted for grinding in leaky sea valves and installation of 2 high pressure globe stop valves in main engine circulating salt water system.

Machinist James Samuel Heist, USN was detached and Lieutenant (jg) Ted Neir Swain, USN, reported aboard. Readiness for sea 3 June 1944.

(B) NARRATIVE

3 June 1944

1330 (VW)Underway from Pearl Harbor, T.H. enroute patrol area via Midway in accordance with Comsaskfor Seventeen Operation Order 191-44 of 1 June 1944.
1530 (VW)Made deep test dive. Results: flooded SJ radar mast; engine salt water sea valves still leaking; both periscopes extremely rough in train; considerable noise and vibration around periscope shears.
1626 (VW)Surfaced. Drained SJ Radar Mast, flushed out with fresh water, dried with air and installed new face plate gasket on parabola housing. Subsequent tests of SJ radar and lobing appeared to be satisfactory. Will have it further checked in Midway.
2000 (VW)Released escort, PC-485; proceeding independently.

15 June 1944

Weather 100% overcast with light haze around horizon, seas were moderate during forenoon.

In afternoon, seas became mountainous with wind building up in intensity and barometer dropping rapidly, such that by nightfall, it became evident we were very near a typhoon. Sent PAMPANITO 1st Serial to ComSubPac, concerning weather conditions.

2028 (K)Made SJ land contact on TORI SHIMA bearing 344° T. - 60,000 yards. Wind and seas moderated after passing through island chain; however, experienced heavy rain squalls throughout the night. Took some water down the main induction and conning tower hatch - with no resultant damage.

16 June 1944

Entering eastern edge of Area 6-A with seas moderate to heavy.

0419 (I)Dived. Decided to stay submerged today in order to train planesmen and officers in depth control and periscope watches. No star sights tonight. Converted #4-A and 4-B from FBT to MDT.
2300 (I)Made trim dive to check operation of #4 vents, etc. Tested satisfactorily.

17 June 1944

Surface patrol in area. Seas moderate to heavy. Heavy rain squalls throughout day and night - no star sights.

23 June 1944

0232 (I)Picking up numerous SJ radar pips at various ranges and bearings. One of these pips looked so good and since there were very few clouds in the sky and seas were glassy smooth, closed to investigate, only to find pip becoming hazy, indicating that it was probably caused by "Ionizated Conditions" of the atmosphere.
0350 (I)C.O.D., Navigator and one lookout sighted a Torpedo Wake crossing our bow. Changed course to left with full rudder at flank speed, paralleling this track only to find another wake proceeding up our starboard side.

The night was clear and I'm sure if there had been a submarine on the surface, we could have seen it. As it turned out, I feel certain that a submerged enemy submarine had fired at us and his misses were due merely to the fact that we were zig-zagging, for which I am now very thankful.
0402 (I)Decided to submerge and see if we can pick up his screws. Headed in his general direction but were unable to pick him up. Patrolling today just south of OKINO SHIMA covering route from BUNGO SUIDO eastward around ASHIZUHA SAKI.

Just before diving this A.M., enemy radar from either or both ASHIZUHA SAKI and OKINO SHIMA settled on us and stayed steady on our bearing until we dove, so they evidently have us pretty well spotted in. (They were using about 97 Megs.)
0715 (I)Sighted small motor sampan bearing 348° T. - distant 3000 yards. Avoided.

0352 (I)Dived to patrol traffic route between ASHIZURI SAKI and MUHOTO SAKI. Visibility is not too good; hazy land in distance to northward.
1129-1448 (I)Encountered numerous small spitkits off point of land, believed at the time to be ASHIZURI SAKI. However, upon surfacing tonight, we found we had been set a distance of over 40 miles during the day in the direction of 065° T. Thus, the spitkits encountered above were actually off MUROTO SAKI.
1749-1807 (I)Heard 8 distant explosions. Periscope observation disclosed all clear.

2030 (I)Picked up enemy radar, first sweeping and then steady on us - probably from SHIONO MISAKI.
2217 (I)SJ Radar reported very small "Pip" bearing 059°, range 12,000 yards (Ship Contact #3). Stationed the tracking party and commenced tracking. Target appears to be on course about 290° T, making 7 or 8 knots. Closed to investigate. Sea is calm, and we are between target and bright moon. When range had closed to about 8000 yards, picked up target with binoculars and identified it either as a small trawler or else a submarine. Decided to try and make an end around and try to place target between us and moon. However on starting end around, target changed course to north and very suddenly disappeared from radar screen. Closed original range to about 5000 yards and could not pick up any definite pip, so decided to give up the idea of further chase, as target was either a small patrol vessel or else a submarine and had dived and was waiting for a shot at us. I believe, however, that he was a patrol craft sent out to look for us after we were picked up by enemy radar.

4 July 1944

1222 (I)Sighted small trawler type patrol vessel bearing 065° T., distant 2500 yards. Avoided.
This trawler heckled us for about 3 hours. Had he been a little larger, I would have fired a torpedo at him, but since he was only about 250 to 300 tons, decided against it. Also there is the possibility that something bigger might be coming through.

(c) WEATHER

The weather was excellent enroute from Midway until about 300 miles east of NANPO SHOTO when we encountered the edge of a typhoon, with mountainous seas, strong wind and a dropping barometer plus frequent heavy rain squalls. After passing north of TOAI SHIMA, the seas and wind moderated, but the uninterred squally weather for the next 24 hours.

The weather in the areas south of Japan was usually mild with frequent rain squalls of short duration and relatively calm and glassy-smooth seas. Visibility conditions varied from fair to poor, mainly due to the hazy conditions that seem to persist with a smooth sea in this area.

Weather enroute to Midway from NANPO SHOTO was in general excellent with light swells and good visibility.

1. The second war patrol of the U.S.S. PAMPANITO was in areas
off the Japanese empire. Thirty-two days were spent in the areas during
which period the PAMPANITO made seven contacts with surface ships worthy
of torpedo attack, but on only one was she able to close within torpedo
range. Three torpedoes were fired on this attack and one hit was heard in
a 5100 ton freighter. Area coverage was good in spite of heavy surface
and air patrol. Surface patrol was strong off the 100 fathom curve, and
caused the PAMPANITO considerable trouble when closing the Coast. Strong
currents in these coastal areas made navigation difficult and submerged
patrol transits.

2. ATTACK NUMBER ONE.

This was a periscope attack on July 6th on a convoy consist-
ing of two medium AK's and one small AC with three escorts. Three Mark
18-1 torpedoes were fired from the stern tubes at one of the escorts, an
Otari type torpedo boat, but the target believed hit was a 5100 ton AK
similar to the Toyokawa Maru class. All three torpedoes were fired with a
2° divergent spread on tracks between 60°s and 67° respectively. Depth settings
were eight feet on numbers 7 and 8 tubes and 10 feet on number 9 tube.
One torpedo explosion was heard about six and one-half minutes after firing
the first torpedo. Sound tracked the torpedoes to the screws of a ship
which stopped after the explosion. Breaking-up noises and popping noises
were heard for forty-five minutes after the explosion. A depth charge attack
prevented making an observation until an hour and a quarter after firing.
At this time only the torpedo boat and a float plane were in sight circling
the area in which the ship was last seen and heard. The length of the tor-
pedo run is exceptionally long for a Mark 18-1 torpedo and coincides very
closely with the time for an end of run explosion. However, since the
Commanding Officer insists that he heard the torpedo hit a ship and the
ship screws stopped, after which breaking-up noises were heard it is be-
lieved that the ship was either sunk or damaged.
