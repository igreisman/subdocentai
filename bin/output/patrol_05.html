Subject: U.S.S. PALPALITO - Report of Fifth War Patrol.

(I) PROLOG

PALPALITO arrived North Wharf, Fremantle, Western Australia, 30 December 1944 from Fourth War Patrol. Assigned to Submarine Division 161 and Submarine Squadron 16 for refit and availability to put forth Repair Unit, Navy #137 for refit. The major work items accomplished during refit were: the elimination of the torpedo tube shutters and shutter lids: the installation of deodor hatches in the after battery after torpedo room and after torpedo room hatches; and the addition of two 20 mm gun shields on the after 20 M/m gun.

Commander KING, L. STIRLING, USN, with seal Captain FRANK G. FENNO, Jr., USN as Commanding Officer on 2 January 1945. Lieutenant Commander LANDON L. DAVIS, Jr., USN, was relieved as Executive Officer by Lieutenant (T) Commander IRWIN S. DAVIS. The new additional senior board member of the crew is ARTHUR T. McBRIDE, USN, who relieved ensign withdrawn. Ensign ARTHUR WILLIAM J. BUSH, USNR, reported on board as Fourth Watch Officer. Departed on 15 January 1945 and having briefed D. A. having duly reported on board for duty on 14 January 1945.

The refit was completed 14 January 1945. Seven days post repair and training during which were reported. in the ready magazine. Fired 4" gun, and fired torpedo exercise torpedoes; were followed by two days loading. Readiness for sea on 23 January 1945.

Subject: U.S.S. SEALION - Report of Fifth War Patrol.

NARRATIVE (Continued)

0029 (I) Had just about checked off three misses when the first torpedo hit and simultaneously the ship disintegrated with the bow going one way, the stern in the opposite direction and part of the ship going straight up. Judging from the intense flames and explosions, this ship was evidently loaded with aviation gasoline. One second we'd close our eyes, the sure to share in the effects of the explosions. The second torpedo probably hit whatever was left to hit. The whole area looked like a Fourth of July celebration we all slightly naked in all this starlight. Escort on starboard quarter and checked firing at us and placed several rounds just over the bridge before we could pull clear on all four main engines. For the next twenty minutes one violent explosion followed another as ship was torn to pieces. The stern sank and the bow set on the finishing touch by exploding beautifully and in technicolor.

0041 (I) All clear of escort.

ATTACK DATA

U.S.S. PARCHE (SS384) TORPEDO ATTACK NO. 1 PATROL NO. 5

Time - 2157(H). Date - 6 February 1945. Lat. - 06° 29' N. Long. - 106° 11.5' E.

Target Data - Damage Inflicted

Description - At 1653 (H) smoke was sighted by the high periscope and tracking determined it was heading north. Set course to starboard to gain a favorable position for a night approach. The smoke proved to be from one ship of a convoy consisting of the following ships: 1 large AK or AP; 1 large AO or AK; 1 medium small burning AK and 1 escort. One of the escorts appeared to be a SHIOCHI type destroyer escort and the others were PC type patrol vessels. The three ships in the convoy were in a line of bearing of about 255° and 1600 yards apart. The large AK or AP was the leading ship, the large AK or AO was the second ship and the medium AK was the third ship. One escort was ahead of the formation, one escort on either flank and escort astern. The night was clear but the moon had not risen. It was too dark for a periscope attack and too light for a close range surface attack.

Ship Sunk - 3 large AK or AP - 7900 tons (AU).

Damage - Determined by - Two torpedo hits were observed from the bridge in the leading ship; one near the stern and the other just aft of amidships superstructure. The ship was then seen to go down by the stern and two escorts closed on this ship. The radar pip decreased in intensity until it disappeared. The remaining pips were tracked for about 30 minutes, but the two escorts (identified by a PIT) were observed by radar to be milling around the same general vicinity apparently picking up survivors. Upon the completion of the third attack we returned to this position and the two escorts were still milling around but there were no signs of the large ship.

Target Course - 040° T. Target Speed - 8 Knots. Target Range - 4300 Yards.

The few days spent in the area and the scarcity of dives gave opportunity for only three bathythermograph card readings. The general health of the officers and men was excellent throughout this patrol.

low altitude. Position was gained ahead about four and half
hours after contact and an attack was made firing a total of
six torpedoes at the two nearest ships in formation. Two
hits were observed in the nearest and apparently largest ship
and ship went down by the stern. Radar pip decreased and
finally disappeared.

(b) Attack No. 2: After the first attack and subsequent evading
tactics which necessitated an end around run, an approach was
made on the two remaining ships which now had two of the
original four escorts with them. A total of six torpedoes
were again fired at these two ships and all missed due to
long range which allowed ships to maneuver and avoid.
